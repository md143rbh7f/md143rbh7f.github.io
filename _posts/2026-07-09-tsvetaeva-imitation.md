## Never
```
I’m glad to know I’m not consumed with you,
And glad to see you’re not consumed with me;
That never will our heavy, snow-tracked boots
Unlace and dance away beneath our feet.
I’m glad that I can always play the fool,
And sing the truth, and never skip a beat—
Nor blame the wine, its breathless evening hue
Now burnished by the brushing of your sleeve.

I’m glad that I can smile, and watch you two
Embrace each other, perfectly at peace;
And you won't burn, in turn, with Heaven's fu-
ry, when I lift my prayer to His cheek.
My will and name will never guide you through
These frozen waves, this ocean of our dreams,
That we might wake one day, to cry: We do!—
And seize a promise we could never keep.

I bless you for your presence: silent, lu-
cid, warm, ideal, and true. For sights unseen:
Our steps that never slake the forest dew,
The sun that never fades upon our leaves,
Our walk that never halts beneath the moon,
The dawn that never breaks beyond our sea.
For I, alas, am not consumed with you—
And you, alas, are not consumed with me.
```

## Translator's Notes

Loosely based on Marina Tsvetaeva's "Mnie nravitsa..." / Мне нравится... (Цветаева М. И.).

This was written years ago, but not published because (1) it's part of a larger work still in progress and (2) because I'm quite aware of how it fails as a translation. Eventually I gave up trying to fix the parts that bother me; it's been years since I took a serious look. So now, here it is. I hope that it at least contributes an interesting idea or image.

As with every so-called translation of poetry, something must be sacrificed. In this case the literal meaning gives way to the mouthfeel: Tsvetaeva's essential musicality. For me her sonic qualities and intuition are the most important. So the piece is perhaps akin to one of Robert Lowell's *Imitations* (which were famously panned by Nabokov).

Initially I struggled between pentameter, as in the original, or tetrameter, English words being shorter than Russian ones. The current choice (pentameter) provides more rhythmic space for words to fit into the desired structure, but also leaves gaps that must be filled by the translator.

There are a number of awkward redundancies. The stock phrases also bother me tremendously. ("Play the fool", "skip a beat", "sights unseen".)

Many parts are simply invention. The "heavy ball of Earth" / "тяжелый шар земной" that drifts away in Tsvetaeva's original shrinks to become, instead, two pairs of animated boots. Microcosmic—but hopefully, at least, a parallel cosmos. Much of the wintry language comes from the fact that when trying to translate this poem (for the usual reason; a girl), I found myself on an airplane, trapped at the gate by a snowstorm. So perhaps земной became зимний, the waves froze, and so on. Likewise the oceanic imagery comes from the aforementioned larger work which I hope to eventually finish.

There is also the question of technology. I wrote this before services like ChatGPT became widely available, and have continued avoiding them for most writing tasks. But it is now conceivable that they can execute a translation far better (and certainly far more quickly) than any human could. I do not think this is a wholly negative thing; after all, it is nice to be able to speak to a librarian that has read far more than any single human could. Still, I am afraid about what the value of these human-written words will be in the future.
